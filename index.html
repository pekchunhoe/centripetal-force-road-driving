? Why does the car turn?+

Changing direction means changing velocity. That needs acceleration toward the centre of the curve. On this flat road, static friction supplies the inward force: Ffriction = Fc = mv²/r.

Grip is limited to μsmg. If the required force exceeds this, the car loses grip and initially continues along its tangent because of inertia. There is no new outward force.

Try it: compare Sunny and Rainy at 15 m/s. Then change mass from 800 to 2,000 kg. Both force magnitudes change together, so the critical speed stays the same. At constant speed, a 15 m curve needs four times the force of a 60 m curve.

Model: a point vehicle on an unbanked road, constant selected speed, no air resistance or longitudinal tyre-force budget. Speed adjustments are instantaneous experimental settings. After grip loss, a two-second tangent-coasting illustration omits kinetic friction and steering dynamics. Playback changes viewing speed only. Reset preserves your conditions.
